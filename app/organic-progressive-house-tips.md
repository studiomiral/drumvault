# Organic & Progressive House Tips

As of 2026-11-20.

## Basics

Organic house sits slower and swung (about 118–120 BPM). Progressive house sits faster, straighter and rolling (about 122–124 BPM).

| | Organic house | Progressive house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BPM | 115–122 (often 118–120) | 120–126 (often 122–124) |
| Keys | Minor, often Phrygian, harmonic minor or Phrygian dominant for an Eastern sound | Minor, plain Aeolian or Dorian |
| Feel | Swung (54–60%), percussive, warm | Straighter, rolling, hypnotic |
| Energy | Slow builds, gentle drops | Long builds, a big breakdown, a filter-driven drop |
| Length | 6–8 min, 32-bar phrases | 6–9 min |

## Bass MIDI

Three patterns cover most tracks: rolling 16ths for progressive, a syncopated pluck for organic, and a sub drone for intros and breakdowns.

### 1. Progressive rolling 16ths

Leave the 16th the kick sits on empty and play the other three.

```
beat:  1 e + a  2 e + a  3 e + a  4 e + a
kick:  X . . .  X . . .  X . . .  X . . .
bass:  . x x x  . x x x  . x x x  . x x x
```

- Mostly root notes. Change pitch every 1–2 bars, sometimes for only the last 16th of a bar.
- Short notes (about 1/32 to 1/16 long). Vary velocity between about 90 and 115 so the filter envelope breathes.
- Variant: drop the `e` (`. . x x`) for a lighter offbeat groove.

### 2. Organic syncopated pluck

A sparser, percussive groove that answers the congas.

```
beat:  1 e + a  2 e + a  3 e + a  4 e + a
bass:  . . x .  . x . .  x . . x  . . x .     (3+3+2 / tresillo-ish)
```

- Use root, octave and fifth. A b7 or b6 as a passing note adds Eastern colour.
- Tie one long note over the bar line every 4 bars. The space matters as much as the notes.

### 3. Sub drone or pedal

Hold the root for 4–8 bars while the chords move above it. It works in intros and breakdowns, and it lets the arp carry the harmony.

### Harmony that works

- Two-chord vamps over 8 bars: i → VI, or i → VII.
- Four-chord loops: i–VI–III–VII, or i–iv–VI–v.
- Keep the bass on the root (or a pedal) while pads use m7, m9, sus2 and add9 voicings.

## Bass presets

Keep the sub a clean mono sine and put the character in the 200–800 Hz range. Logic stock instruments come first, third-party options in brackets.

| Sound | Synth | Recipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rolling progressive bass | Retro Synth or ES2 (Diva, Serum) | Saw + sine sub one octave down, mono, no glide. 24 dB low-pass at about 300–600 Hz, moderate envelope amount, decay about 80–150 ms, sustain 0. Light saturation, strong kick sidechain. |
| Organic pluck bass | Alchemy or Sculpture (any FM synth) | Sine carrier, modulator at ratio 2:1 or 1:1, short FM envelope: a woody, marimba-like thump. Or a filtered Moog-style square through tape saturation. |
| Darker progressive (Reese) | ES2, Alchemy (Serum) | Two saws detuned about ±10 cents, band-passed, slow LFO on the cutoff. Keep the true sub as a separate sine. |
| Sub drone | Any synth, sine | Pure sine, slow attack, long release, sits under pads in breakdowns. |

For all of them: mono below about 120 Hz, and duck the bass consistently against the kick.

## Other synths and MIDI

In progressive house the arp drives the track. In organic house, pads and plucks largely replace house-style chord stabs.

- **Arp or sequence.** A 16th-note sequence that repeats every 3 or 5 steps against the 4/4 grid always feels like it's moving. Use a 1/8-dotted ping-pong delay, and automate the low-pass cutoff over 16–32 bars. That sweep is your build.
- **Plucks.** Kalimba, marimba or mallet tones from Sculpture or Alchemy, with short decays and a big hall reverb. Write call and response: 2 bars of call, 2 of answer.
- **Pads.** Slow attack (0.5–2 s), filtered, evolving through slow wavetable or LFO movement. String ensembles and choir layers suit breakdowns.
- **Lead or hook.** Sparse, often an "ethnic" timbre: flute, ney, duduk, oud, handpan, or a soft detuned saw. Pentatonic minor, harmonic minor and Phrygian dominant give the Café de Anatolia / Bedouin flavour.
- **Texture.** Field recordings (forest, rain, birds), vinyl or tape noise, granular drones, reversed reverb swells. These make a track sound organic more than anything else.
- **Drums (organic).** A rounder, softer kick, then congas, bongos, shakers, toms, tambourine and roomy claps, with ride used sparingly. Swing the percussion, not the kick.
- **Drums (progressive).** A tight kick, rolling 16th hats, an offbeat open hat and a ride that comes in for the main sections.

## Don't forget

Go through this list before you finish or upload a track.

- [ ] **Sample licences.** Most royalty-free licences let you use a sample inside a track but forbid sharing it on its own. A stem that is basically a raw pack loop or vocal can break that, especially with vocals and "ethnic" loops. Also check what House of Tracks requires.
- [ ] **Tuning.** World-instrument loops are often slightly off A440 or mislabelled. Check them against key detection before you trust the filename.
- [ ] **DJ-friendly intro and outro.** 16–32 bars of drums and percussion only, with no tonal bass, so DJs can mix in and out.
- [ ] **Leave space.** Change one element every 8–16 bars and let automation carry the story.
- [ ] **Mono and low end.** Keep everything below about 120 Hz mono, check the mix in mono, and keep the ducking consistent.
- [ ] **Loudness.** Aim for about −8 to −7 LUFS integrated, not crushed.
- [ ] **Genre tag.** On Beatport, Organic House and Progressive House are separate genres. Pick one per track.
- [ ] **Camelot key in the metadata.** Progressive DJs mix harmonically.
